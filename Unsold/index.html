The Unsold Follow-Up Process

Every call where the homeowner said "not today" is a warm lead sitting in your software. This is the exact call your customer service manager makes to find out why, fix anything that went wrong, and get the work back on the calendar.

Phone scriptVoicemail scriptEmail scriptFill in once, use every day

Why this call works

It doesn't sound like a sales call because it isn't one. It's a service check, and it opens the door to the options the homeowner passed on.

It comes from the office

A customer service manager, not the tech. The homeowner gets to be honest about what happened in their home.

It leads with service

"Did you get everything you paid for?" earns the right to ask about anything else.

It asks why, not "will you buy"

You learn what held them back, then offer to have someone come back and re-look at it.

It ends on the calendar

Every branch ends one of two ways: a booked visit or a booked callback.

Script 1

The phone call

Read the white lines word for word. Green is where the call is going well. Red is where you slow down and listen.

Open

"Hey {cust}, this is {me}, the customer service manager over at {co}. How are you doing today? I'm following up to make sure you got everything you paid for on your most recent visit from {tech}. Do you have a couple minutes to make sure you got everything you paid for?"

If no"Ok, when would be a better time and day for me to reach back out to you?" BOOK THE CALLBACK
Service check

"Thank you so much for your time. What did you think about the level of service {tech} provided to you?"

If good"Fantastic! Thank you so much for those kind words. Do you mind if I let {tech} know the nice things you said about the visit?"
If bad"I appreciate you letting me know, because this is exactly why I do my job. To make sure every client's experience meets or exceeds the {co} standard, can you tell me more about your experience so we can get it corrected?"
Confirm the reason

"I noticed the reason {tech} visited your home was because of {reason}. Is that correct?"

If that's not right"Ok, if you don't mind me asking, what was the main thing you were hoping to accomplish with our visit?"
Then: "Thank you for clarifying. Our goal is to always make sure you have a 5-star experience. Would you be open to us scheduling a time to come out and re-address that?" BOOK THE CALL
Name the concern

"I see that during the visit {tech} had a concern with {concern} in the {room}."

Ask why not

"The reason {tech} offered that was to {benefit}. If you don't mind me asking, {cust}, what was the reason you didn't go with that?"

Then"We really do feel like {option} would help with the comfort, safety and reliability of your home and family. Just so we know how to explain it better in the future, what would have made you want to get it taken care of?"
Then"Would you be open to having one of our quality control specialists come out and re-look at the project, to see if we can make it work for you?" BOOK THE CALL
Recap and close

"Ok {cust}, before we finish I'd like to do a quick review so we're set to serve you best..."

  • "We have you on the calendar on [day] between [time window], where our certified electrician will help take care of this and brighten your day."
  • "We'll call the day before to confirm the appointment, and send you a text or call when we're on our way."
  • "Do you have any questions at this point?"
  • "Ok {cust}, on behalf of the entire team here at {co}, I'd like to thank you for the opportunity to help you with this. We really look forward to working with you soon. Have an amazing day!"
Coach's eye: when you name the concern and what fixing it does, use homeowner language, not trade terms. Not sure how to say something? Look it up in the Field Guide translation tables.
Script 2

No answer? Leave this voicemail

Short, friendly, and about their service, not a sale. Then send the email the same day.

Voicemail
  1. "Hello {cust}, this is {me}, the customer service manager over at {co}.
  2. I noticed {tech} came out to your home on {date} for {reason}.
  3. The reason I was calling is to make sure {tech} gave you 5-star service and you got everything you paid for.
  4. I'm here in the office between {hours}. When you get the chance, please give me a call back at {phone}. I look forward to hearing from you.
  5. Thank you for your time and have a great day."
Script 3

The follow-up email

Send it after the voicemail so they have your number in writing.

Email

Hello {cust},

This is {me}, the customer service manager at {co}.

The reason I'm reaching out is that my role is to make sure our clients get a 5-star experience when we visit their home, and to take care of any concerns they might have had.

I took some time to review the options that were designed for you, and I see some ways we can make them work even better for you. When you get a chance, please give me a call at {phone} between {hours}, or let me know a time and day that works best for us to touch base.

Have a blessed day,

{me}
Customer Service Manager, {co}
