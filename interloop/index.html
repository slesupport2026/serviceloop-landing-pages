04 · the machine

Here's why this keeps working after six weeks.

Not what's in it. How it works. Because once you can see the machine, everything else about this decision gets simple.

Start with the thing nobody says out loud about information.

A gym membership is the easy part. It gets you the room, the equipment and the door code. Nobody in history has been held back by not having access to a squat rack.

And you know exercises. You've known some for years. You could walk in tonight and put together a real workout without asking a soul.

So why does almost every membership die by February?

Not because the exercises stopped working. Because nobody was expecting you Tuesday. Nobody watched the rep and told you your back was rounding. And nobody moved the weight up before you'd have moved it yourself.

Everybody plateaus. That isn't a character flaw, it's physiology. The body adapts to the load it already knows.

The word for the thing that actually builds people is progressive overload. Somebody loading the bar heavier than you would have loaded it, on a schedule, for years. That's it. That's the entire trick.

Not more exercises. Not better information. Someone who knows your name, expects you on Tuesday, corrects the technique while you're still under the bar, and keeps adding plates well past the point where you'd have called it a good session.

Night School handed you the movements. Good ones. You ran them for six weeks and you got stronger, and you have the numbers to prove it.

The Inner Loop is the coach, the schedule, and the plate that isn't on the bar yet.

Look at who actually has coaches. It's never the guy who wandered in off the street. It's the pros. Every one of them.

Jordan had coaches. Brady had coaches. The people with the least time available and the most to lose from wasting it all landed on the same conclusion. And the coach usually can't lift what the athlete lifts — that was never the job. The job is seeing what you can't see from under the bar, and refusing to let you sit at a weight you've already mastered.

So here's the whole machine.

Five moving parts. That's all there is to it, and there's nothing clever about any of them.

Game Plan→Action→Accountability→Progressive overload→Repeat
—

The Game Plan names the constraint.

Not a wish list. The one thing holding the business back right now, decided with somebody who has removed it before.

—

Action happens on the jobs you're already running.

Not extra work bolted on the side. The same calls, the same crew, the same week — done to a standard that was decided in advance.

—

The room becomes accountability.

Not pressure. Expectation. There's a difference, and you can feel it in your chest.

—

Progressive overload keeps the load rising.

The next Game Plan is heavier than the last one, because you are. That's the part that compounds, and it's the part you cannot do for yourself.

—

Then it repeats.

Through the slow months, through the bad hire, through the stretch where you'd otherwise have quietly gone back to how it was in April.

That's it. That's the operating system. Nine turns of it a year, for as long as you keep winning.

Every Game Plan identifies the next business constraint. Every cycle you improve the business. Every cycle the standard rises.

Not because you're motivated. Motivation is weather. Because the machine keeps moving.

It isn't random coaching. It isn't whatever's on the agenda that week. It's the same diagnostic run on your business every six weeks, finding the one thing holding the whole thing back, and removing it.

Which is why it doesn't matter what breaks next. The method doesn't change when the problem does.

And now notice what the machine doesn't ask of you.

The Inner Loop isn't another thing sitting beside your business. It becomes how you keep improving the business you're already running.

Night School probably didn't give you more hours. It gave those hours somewhere to go. The work was happening anyway — this is the part that gives it direction.

The Handshake.

Before anything else, let's clear up the thing this page has been implying for nine sections.

Twelve months. That's the whole ask.

The agreement is twelve months. At the end of it, you decide again. We'd like to do that ten times. We're not going to make you do it twice.

Some of the men on this page have signed it four and five times over. One year at a time, every time, because they kept winning. That's a description of what happened in here — not a term we're asking you to accept.

There is a document. We'll both sign it, and it's short. But you already know us and we already know you, so let's shoot straight about what actually holds this together.

We're not going to lock you in. We'd rather you locked yourself in.

So here's the handshake. Read it as both halves — every line runs in both directions.

01

Service to others.

We keep you top of mind. You keep your people, your customers and this room top of mind. That's the order of operations, and everything else here comes out of it.

02

Imperfect action.

It will be messy. You'll run a cycle that doesn't land. We're not going to pretend otherwise and neither should you. Move anyway.

03

Accountability, not babysitting.

We can't make you do anything and we won't try. What we'll do is expect you. When you're off your best, say so — that's the whole mechanism. A missed week isn't a problem. A quiet one is.

04

A hundred and a hundred.

The best relationships aren't fifty-fifty. We bring everything we've got, you bring everything you've got, and nobody keeps score, because both columns are full.

05

Money, clear and out of the way.

Simple businesses get to focus on service, so we settle the financial side up front and then stop thinking about it. We both agree the value exceeds the price, and that the target is ten times what you put in — your time, your energy and your hard-earned dollars. If it isn't shaping up that way, we say so out loud, early.

06

What's said in the room stays in the room.

Real numbers. Real fears. Real marriages. Men in here talk about things they don't say anywhere else, and that is precisely why it works.

07

Health first. Family second. Business third.

We hold each other to that order — including when you're the one trying to break it. Burn out and everything else on this page becomes theoretical.

And when we fall down.

The best of friends, when one of them goes down, don't file a report. They put a hand out and pick him up.

That's the intention here, and we'd rather say it now than the first time you need it.

There is never a reason for shame between us. Only a reason to communicate, tell the truth, and honour this handshake.
