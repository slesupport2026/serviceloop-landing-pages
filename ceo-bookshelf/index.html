The CEO Bookshelf

The books behind the businesses. Hand-picked from our own shelves for Service Pros building something bigger than a job.

39 BOOKS  ·  10 SHELVES  ·  1 MISSION

Guys. Real talk for a second.

Nobody handed me a playbook when I started. My first business ended with me pulling my last few thousand bucks out of the account with tears rolling down my cheek. What turned it around wasn't talent. It was learning from people who'd already made the mistakes I was busy making.

Every book on this list earned its spot. These aren't theory books for MBA types. They're the ones that changed how we price, how we serve, how we lead, and how we think. Read them slow. Take notes. Then take action — because a highlighted book that changed nothing is just a decorated shelf. Right?

— Clay NeumeyerCEO & Co-Founder, Service Loop Electrical

01

Don't read in order

Go to the shelf that matches your biggest fire right now. Pricing problem? Strategy shelf. Team problem? Leadership shelf.

02

Start with the badges

Books marked Read First are the fastest wins — start there if you're new to the reading game.

03

One idea, one action

After every book, pick ONE idea and run the play in your business within 7 days. That's the whole system.

Sales & Closing

Selling means serving. These three will change what happens at the kitchen table.

Never Split the Difference

Chris VossRead FirstGet it on Amazon →

A former FBI hostage negotiator breaks down how real negotiation works — and it's not about splitting anything down the middle. Voss teaches tactical empathy: label what the other person feels, ask calibrated questions, and let them talk their way to yes.

Look forward toThe "mirroring" technique — repeat the last three words someone says and watch them open up. You'll use it on your very next service call.

Clay's Take

"That's right" beats "you're right" every single time. When a homeowner says "that's right," they've sold themselves. Our job is just to get them there. K?

The Challenger Sale

Matthew DixonGet it on Amazon →

Based on a massive study of top sales performers, this book found the best sellers aren't relationship-builders — they're challengers who teach the customer something new, tailor the message, and take control of the conversation.

Look forward toWhy "the customer is always right" is costing you money, and how leading with an insight beats leading with a discount.

Clay's Take

Homeowners don't need another order-taker in their kitchen. They need a pro who shows them what they didn't know about their own home. Teach first. The ticket follows.

Door-to-Door Millionaire

Lenny GrayGet it on Amazon →

Straight from the trenches of door-to-door sales — the hardest selling environment there is. Gray covers first impressions, handling brush-offs, and the discipline of doing the reps when nobody's watching.

Look forward toObjection-handling lines you can use word-for-word. If it works on a cold doorstep, it works everywhere.

Clay's Take

More swings at bat, more grand slams. This book is proof that sales is a numbers game played with skill. No shortcuts. Just reps.

Marketing & Leads

A full board beats a full toolbox. Here's how the phone starts ringing.

$100M Offers

Alex HormoziRead FirstGet it on Amazon →

Hormozi's formula for building offers so good people feel stupid saying no. It's about stacking value, killing risk, and pricing with confidence — not competing on being the cheapest guy with a van.

Look forward toThe value equation. Four levers that let you raise prices while making the deal BETTER for the customer.

Clay's Take

If you're the cheapest electrician in town, this book will make you uncomfortable. Good. I'm begging you not to fear the increase — build an offer worth paying for instead.

$100M Leads

Alex HormoziGet it on Amazon →

The follow-up to Offers, covering the four ways strangers find out you exist: warm outreach, content, cold outreach, and paid ads. Simple frameworks, real math, zero fluff.

Look forward toThe "give away the secrets, sell the implementation" play — the exact reason we run free workshops.

Clay's Take

Leads aren't luck. They're a system. Pick ONE of the four channels, get good at it, then add the next. Complexity is the enemy of execution.

Building a StoryBrand 2.0

Donald MillerGet it on Amazon →

Miller's framework makes your customer the hero and your company the guide. If your website talks about you instead of the homeowner's problem, this book explains why nobody's calling.

Look forward toThe 7-part story framework you can apply to your website in one afternoon and watch the difference.

Clay's Take

Here's the thing — the homeowner is Luke Skywalker. We're Yoda. The moment your marketing figures that out, everything gets easier.

No B.S. Trust Based Marketing

Dan S. KennedyGet it on Amazon →

Kennedy's guide to marketing in a world where nobody trusts anybody. Position yourself as the trusted authority in your area, and price resistance starts to disappear.

Look forward toWhy being the "hyperlocal hero" beats being everywhere — and how trust lets you charge what you're worth.

Clay's Take

Trust is the whole ballgame in the trades. People let us into their homes, near their kids, around their pets. Market like you understand what that means.

Launch

Walker's Product Launch Formula — building anticipation before you sell anything. Sequences, pre-launch content, and turning an announcement into an event people wait for.

Look forward toThe "sideways sales letter" concept: sell through a sequence of value instead of one desperate pitch.

Clay's Take

Launching a service agreement program? A new install offer? Don't just quietly add it to the truck. Build it up. Make it an event. Watch what happens.

Influence & Communication

Before we sell, we see. Before we tell, we're told. This shelf is the how.

Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion

Robert CialdiniGet it on Amazon →

The godfather of persuasion science. Cialdini's principles — reciprocity, scarcity, authority, consistency, liking, social proof — explain why people say yes, backed by decades of research.

Look forward toSpotting these principles everywhere once you know them — in every ad, every pitch, every review request.

Clay's Take

This isn't manipulation, guys. It's understanding people. Use it to serve at a higher level and the profits pile up on their own.

How to Win Friends and Influence People

Dale CarnegieGet it on Amazon →

Nearly a century old and still undefeated. Remember names, listen more than you talk, make people feel important — the fundamentals of being someone people want to work with.

Look forward toHow the simplest habits — a name remembered, a genuine compliment — compound into a reputation.

Clay's Take

Old book. Timeless plays. If every tech on your team ran even half of this, your review count would double. I promise you.

Cues

The science of charisma — body language, vocal tone, and the small signals that decide whether people read you as warm, competent, both, or neither. Practical and immediately usable.

Look forward toThe warmth-and-competence matrix. You'll rewatch your own sales videos differently after this one.

Clay's Take

Your customer decides a lot about you before you say a word at the door. This book shows you what they're reading. Does that make sense? It will.

Endless Referrals

Bob BurgGet it on Amazon →

Burg's system for turning every contact into a referral source — not through gimmicks, but by becoming genuinely valuable to your network first. Give first, and the pipeline builds itself.

Look forward toFeel-good questions that get anyone talking about their business — and remembering yours.

Clay's Take

Referrals are the cheapest leads you'll ever get and the best customers you'll ever have. This is the system for earning them on purpose instead of by accident.

Leadership & Team

You can't scale past yourself until you can lead past yourself.

Extreme Ownership

Jocko WillinkGet it on Amazon →

Two Navy SEALs translate combat leadership into business. The core: everything in your world is your responsibility. No blaming the market, the techs, or the customer. Own it all.

Look forward to"There are no bad teams, only bad leaders" — a chapter that will sting and then set you free.

Clay's Take

The day you stop blaming the helper, the supplier, and the economy is the day your company starts growing. Own it all. Every bit of it.

Multipliers

Liz WisemanGet it on Amazon →

Some leaders drain intelligence from their teams; others multiply it. Wiseman shows how "diminishers" accidentally shrink their people — and how multipliers get 2x from the same crew.

Look forward toThe "accidental diminisher" checklist. Warning: you will see yourself in it.

Clay's Take

If you're the smartest person in every conversation at your shop, you built it that way. This book is how you fix it — and finally take a real vacation.

Start with Why

Simon SinekGet it on Amazon →

People don't buy what you do — they buy why you do it. Sinek's Golden Circle explains why some companies inspire loyalty from customers and employees while others just transact.

Look forward toFiguring out your own why — the thing that gets techs to stay and customers to refer.

Clay's Take

Guys, "we do electrical work" isn't a why. Ours is service to others. When your team knows the why, the how gets a whole lot easier to lead.

Daring Greatly

Brené BrownGet it on Amazon →

Brown's research on vulnerability and courage. Real leadership means showing up when you can't control the outcome — admitting mistakes, having hard conversations, and building trust through honesty.

Look forward toWhy the leader who says "I don't know, let's figure it out" builds a stronger crew than the one who fakes it.

Clay's Take

We're just a bunch of electricians with honesty. That's not a weakness — it's the best way we're gonna break through. This book explains why that works.

Mindset & Performance

Look forward toThe Cooler / Closer / Cleaner framework — and the honest question of which one you actually are.

Winning

Grover's follow-up: winning is a full-price purchase, paid in advance, no discounts. Thirteen hard truths about what it actually costs to be the best at anything.

Look forward to"Winning is not a marathon, it's a sprint with no finish line" — and twelve more truths like it.

Think and Grow Rich

Napoleon HillGet it on Amazon →

The 1937 classic built from studying hundreds of the era's most successful people. Definite purpose, burning desire, a mastermind circle — the original blueprint most modern business books rewrote.

Look forward toThe mastermind principle. It's the reason communities like ours exist.

Almost a hundred years old and it still hits. The mastermind chapter alone is worth it — nobody builds a 7-figure shop alone. Nobody.

Hidden Potential

Adam GrantGet it on Amazon →

Grant's research on how far people can go from unremarkable starting points. Character skills beat raw talent, discomfort is a growth signal, and great systems create great performers.

Look forward toWhy "the best students rarely become the best performers" — and what that means for who you hire.

Clay's Take

Grade 10 math, maybe a dropout — if you're like me, this book is proof that where you started means nothing. It's about distance traveled, guys.

10x Is Easier Than 2x

Dan Sullivan & Benjamin HardyGet it on Amazon →

Counterintuitive and true: 2x goals keep you grinding harder at what you already do. 10x goals force you to drop 80% of it and focus only on what actually moves the needle.

Look forward toThe 80/20 identity shift — letting go of the work that built you to make room for the work that scales you.

Clay's Take

Doubling down on more calls and longer days is 2x thinking. 10x means asking what you'd have to STOP doing. That question changed how I run everything.

Feel the Fear and Do It Anyway

Susan JeffersGet it on Amazon →

Fear never fully goes away — and that's fine. Jeffers reframes fear as a signal you're growing, and gives simple tools for acting while scared instead of waiting for confidence that never comes.

Look forward to"The fear will never go away as long as I continue to grow" — the sentence that makes the price increase possible.

Clay's Take

Scared to raise your rates? Scared to hire? Good. That's the feeling of leveling up. Feel it, then run the play anyway.

Strategy & Scale

From sparkie to operator. The 5-year path forward starts on this shelf.

Buy Back Your Time

Dan MartellRead FirstGet it on Amazon →

Martell's rule: don't hire to grow the business, hire to buy back your time — then use that time on what only you can do. Includes the exact playbook for your first real hires.

Look forward toThe "buyback rate" calculation that tells you exactly which tasks you should never be doing again.

Clay's Take

If you're still doing your own invoicing at $500K a year, this book is an intervention. Your hourly rate in the truck is not your hourly rate as a CEO.

The Science of Scaling

Benjamin HardyGet it on Amazon →

Hardy applies psychology research to growth: your business scales when your identity, standards, and environment scale first. The bottleneck is almost never tactics — it's the operator's frame.

Look forward toWhy raising your floor (your minimum standard) beats chasing a higher ceiling.

Clay's Take

We don't rise to our training. We fall to our standards. This book is the science behind why setting a higher floor changes everything above it.

Your Next Five Moves

Patrick Bet-DavidGet it on Amazon →

Business as chess. Bet-David walks through knowing who you want to be, reasoning through moves in advance, and building the team and systems to execute — always thinking five moves out.

Look forward toThe "solve for X" method for isolating the real problem instead of the loud one.

Clay's Take

Most of us run our shops one move at a time — react, react, react. Five moves out is where the calm comes from. That's the whole 5-year path idea.

The Road Less Stupid

Keith J. CunninghamGet it on Amazon →

Cunningham's argument: you don't need more genius, you need fewer dumb mistakes. His "thinking time" practice — sitting with hard questions on a schedule — is worth more than any tactic.

Look forward toThe question sets. Some of the best business questions ever put on paper.

Clay's Take

Operating costs money. Thinking is free. Thirty scheduled minutes with a legal pad has saved me from more expensive mistakes than any course I've bought.

The Diary of a CEO: 33 Laws

Steven BartlettGet it on Amazon →

Bartlett distills hundreds of interviews with world-class performers into 33 laws of business and life. Short chapters, big ideas — from "master the useless first" to "you must out-fail the competition."

Look forward toLaw-sized chapters you can read one per morning with your coffee. Perfect for busy operators.

Clay's Take

Easy to read, hard to ignore. Keep it in the truck. One law a day beats one binge a year — small percents adding up, guys.

Buy Then Build

Wealth & Finance

The business builds the wealth. The mindset decides if you keep it.

Profit First

Mike MichalowiczRead FirstGet it on Amazon →

Michalowicz flips the old formula. Instead of Sales − Expenses = Profit, it becomes Sales − Profit = Expenses. Take your profit first, in a separate account, and force the business to run on what's left. Built on human behavior, not accounting theory — which is exactly why it works.

Look forward toThe bank account system — five accounts, two transfer days a month, and the "smaller plates" trick that makes overspending nearly impossible.

Clay's Take

Profit isn't what's left over at the end of the year, guys. It's the first bill you pay. Set up the accounts this week — small percents adding up is the whole game. This one's non-negotiable on my list.

The Science of Getting Rich

Wallace D. WattlesGet it on Amazon →

A 1910 classic on wealth as a creative act, not a competitive one. Wattles argues for creating value rather than fighting over scraps — with a mental discipline that's aged surprisingly well.

Look forward toThe creation-versus-competition mindset — the difference between undercutting the guy down the street and building something he can't copy.

Clay's Take

Over a century old. Still true: there's no shortage of work, only a shortage of value created. Stop competing on price. Start creating.

The Illusion of Money

Kyle CeaseGet it on Amazon →

A challenge to the belief that money will fix how you feel. Cease argues that chasing the number from a place of fear costs you the exact creativity and presence that create real wealth.

Look forward toAn uncomfortable question: what would you build if you weren't scared about money? Probably the thing you should be building.

Clay's Take

Revenue goals matter. But if you hit a million and you're still anxious, still grinding, still missing dinner — did you win? This one's about the wealth behind the wealth.
