The Monthly Event Engine

One 28-day loop. Four rooms. Every door leads to Night School. This doc explains what we're building, how the month flows, who owns what, and why this is easier than what we're doing now — not harder. Read it once, then run the play.

Why this exists

We've been creating the value already — Joe's classes, the YouTube content, the cohorts. We just weren't marketing it. The Event Engine fixes that with one repeating monthly rhythm instead of one-off pushes.

01

It's a bow-tie, not a funnel

A normal funnel ends at the sale. Ours mirrors itself: the same monthly events that pull prospects in also serve clients after they buy. No more awkwardly dragging clients back into the lead funnel — retention and acquisition run on the same calendar.

02

Irrefutably needed

The goal of Night School is to be the obvious answer. A masterclass, a bootcamp, and a workshop every single month means there's always a live room, always a next step, and always threads to talk about for the rest of the month.

03

One machine, not three webinars

Don't confuse yourself: there is one masterclass a month (Joseph) and one workshop a month (Clay). The bootcamp and cohort are delivery, not promotion. Call things what they are.

Calendar per event — the 30-day runway

Each live event is really a month-long push. The day this month's event fires, the next content wrapper drops, the long-form video releases, and it gets quartered — each quarter feeds one week of 2–3 short-form pieces, all guiding people to next month's live event. We are always trailing 30 days ahead of the room.

The wrapper system

A "content wrapper" is the monthly theme package: the topic, the metaphor, the thumbnail, the angle. Everything downstream — emails, landing page updates, posts, ads — gets generated from it. The rule: every live event gets a trailing 30-day runway. The moment this month's event fires, the next wrapper drops and its runway starts — we are never not building toward the next room.

  1. Clay builds the wrapper — topic, metaphor, imagery. The one creative decision per month.
  2. The bot runs it — PostPal becomes the MasterClass wrapper and the Workshop wrapper. It already has the RAG folders, Joseph's voice, Clay's voice. It drafts the 7-email sequence, page copy, posts, and ad copy from the swipe-file templates.
  3. Human review — Clay and Joe spend ~1 hour each reviewing and approving. Daniel confirms links, workflows, and that Revio has context.
  4. Monday huddle — wrapper focus, content calendar, and outgoing emails sent to the team before Monday so everyone's on the same page. Sets go out. Run the play.

"The only thing in the way of this is not know-how. It's putting some time aside and doing it." — and once it's built, nobody can touch it. Others will copy it. We'll still be first on the scene.

Set it & forget it vs. fresh every month

Half the machine never changes. That's the design — it's why this gets easier over time, not harder.

Static — build once, run forever

Bootcamp & Night School
  • Persistent everything: descriptions, landing pages, links, and ads stay the same month to month. No rebuilds, no re-wiring.
  • The Bootcamp landing page carries the rotating focus calendar — click a button to see what the next session covers. The page itself never changes.
  • Night School's pages, application flow, and evergreen ads run untouched. Applications stay open all month, every month.
  • Only minor seasonal tweaks needed — and Claude manages and updates these for us, so no one's bandwidth gets eaten.
  • No launch weight: these are repeated efforts — follow the same steps, one focus week a month for Bootcamp, one for Night School. Get people in, warm the funnel, keep going.

Dynamic — new wrapper monthly

MasterClass & Workshop
  • These two carry the freshness: a new content wrapper each month — new topic, new metaphor, new imagery.
  • Each gets a monthly refresh: landing page update, the 7-email sequence, social posts, and ad creative — all drafted by the wrapper bot, reviewed by a human.
  • Joseph owns the MasterClass wrapper. Clay owns the Workshop wrapper. One creative decision each, per month.
  • Everything else flexes around these two — which is why the monthly lift is roughly one hour of review each for Clay and Joseph.

Straight answers

Does this hurt cohort signups?

It feeds them. 10 front-end signups with 5 backing into Night School beats 20 with 2. Every event creates a warm, already-paid list for Phil to work — and Night School is the answer to every "how do I actually do this?" question the events raise.

Are we giving away too much?

The pattern always runs the other way: the more we put in front of them, the more they say "I can't figure this out without you guys." The best way to work through the complexity is with us — that's exactly what the Inner Loop schedule is.

Does this put more sales calls on Daniel?

No — by design. A $97 MasterClass is a downsell, not a sales call. The only sales calls come from Night School applications, where the call is natural. The trend line is fewer sales calls overall, not more.

What if someone sees two CTAs at once?

Who cares. The funnel stages still apply — this is duplication of content within each stage, not competing offers. More open doors means more ways in.

Isn't the backend a ton of work?

The hard part is what we're doing right now: designing the flow once. After that, the bootcamp is static, the email campaign repeats with a topic swap, and the wrapper bot drafts everything. Pre-write 21 emails (3 per topic) and rotate — nobody remembers an email from 9 months ago.

Will something break?

Probably — there are a lot of moving pieces and quick turnarounds. That's fine. Present, not perfect. Launch, learn, pivot. Patience with the machine while we build it is part of the plan.
