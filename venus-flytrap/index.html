The Venus Flytrap

How to build a Facebook audience that buys — without running a single ad campaign.

Issued September 2026 · Rev. 3

Why this exists

You don't need an ad account. You need an audience.

Most electricians who say they "run Facebook ads" are doing one thing: paying Meta for lead form fills. It works until it doesn't. The leads get worse, the cost climbs, and the day you shut the spend off, everything stops cold. You spent all that money and you own nothing.

The Venus Flytrap is the opposite. You use small boosted posts to pull homeowners onto your page, warm them up over a few weeks, then make them an offer. At the end you own the audience. Turn the money off and the followers are still sitting there.

It's simpler than an ad account. It's cheaper. And you can run the whole thing yourself from your phone.

"I never did well with boosted posts when I was still in the field — boosting like any amateur out there. Because I never had a complete strategy."

Clay Neumeyer, Service Loop Electrical

That's the difference. Boosting isn't the strategy. The order you boost things in is the strategy.

The mechanic

Facebook is an attention highway

Here's the part nobody explains to you.

Facebook gives you, the person, a house right on the highway. You can walk out front and put a sign up every day, and most of your friends will drive past and see it. Free.

But it makes your business sit behind the house. No frontage. A business post gets maybe a quarter of the reach your personal post gets — often less. And every single time you post from the business page, Facebook pops up and asks: do you want to boost this post?

That's not a trick. That's the toll for getting your business out to the highway. The Venus Flytrap is just paying that toll in the right order.

Before you start

Three rules that make or break it

1. Everything runs off the business page.

Your personal page might get better engagement today. Doesn't matter. You can't boost from a personal profile and you can't build a retargetable audience there.

2. Never hit "Share" from your personal page to the business page.

A share is a dead post. Almost no reach, and you can't boost it. Instead: copy the text, re-upload the photo or video, and post it fresh on the business page. Forty extra seconds, and it's the difference between 200 views and 20,000.

3. You are not selling in stage one. You're collecting.

If your first boosted post has a phone number and a discount in it, you built a bad ad, not a flytrap. Selling comes in stage three — after they already know who you are.

1

Top of funnel

The Magnet

Goal: get strangers in your service area to follow the page.

One piece of genuinely useful, local, free content. Not an ad. Something a homeowner actually wants to keep.

  • Every rebate and energy credit available in your state and utility district this year
  • "The 7 things in your panel your home inspector never looks at"
  • A straight explainer on what's really driving their power bill
  • A local meme or a genuinely funny jobsite moment — engagement is engagement

The rebate guide is the strongest play, because it's local, it's specific, and nobody else bothered to make it. Sit down with Claude or ChatGPT, feed it your state and your utilities, and have it pull the current programs together. Then check the numbers yourself before you publish. Your name is on it.

Or do what we did — take the post that already won organically and put it to work. Don't guess at what people want. Look at what already got shared 3,600 times, and pay to show it to more people.

How to post it

  • Put the guide, or the link to it, right in the caption
  • End with a direct ask: "Follow the page — we post this stuff every week."
  • No phone number. No "call for a free estimate." Not yet.

Your target is a few hundred to a couple thousand new local followers in that first month. Treat that as the direction you're steering, not a promise — cost per follower swings with your market and how good the post is. Judge the post, not the plan.

2

Middle of funnel

Build the Trust

Goal: those new followers know your name. Now make them trust you — before you ask for a dollar.

You're now boosting to your own followers only. That's the whole trick. Tiny budget, warm audience, nothing wasted on strangers.

Proof you're legit

  • Local awards and readers'-choice wins
  • Real reviews, screenshotted
  • Your crew, your trucks, your community involvement
  • Years in business, licensing, who you actually are

Proof the problem is real

  • A burned exterior panel you pulled last month
  • A Zinsco or Federal Pacific breaker that didn't trip
  • Scorched wiring behind a receptacle
  • Walk through what you found and what it would have caused

The hazard posts are the engine. But there's a line and it matters: you're educating, not scaring. Explain what it is, why it happens, what to look for. Don't imply their house burns down tonight. Homeowners can smell fearmongering, and it costs you the trust you just spent thirty days buying.

And get your facts straight before you post. Electrical fires are a top-three cause of house fires — not number one. Overstate it and one commenter with Google torches your credibility in public.

Budget
~$4/day per post
Audience
People who already follow your page
Cadence
2–4 of these per month · roughly $120/mo
3

Bottom of funnel

The Offer

Goal: now you ask.

By now your followers have gotten something useful from you, seen your work, seen your awards, and seen what a bad panel actually looks like. The resistance is already gone. You are not cold-calling anybody.

  • Panel Awareness Month — name the specific brands and conditions you're pulling out of houses in your area, then make an offer on inspections or replacements
  • A seasonal offer tied to something real — storm season, holiday load, AC season
  • A flat-rate safety inspection at a clear price

Same small daily budget, same follower audience. Run it for a defined window and stop. An offer that runs forever isn't an offer.

Then go back to Stage 1 and feed the top again.

The comments

Somebody's going to be a jerk

  • Trolls: ignore them, or delete if it's quick. Never argue in public. Nobody has ever won one of those.
  • Competitors showing up to stir things: hide the comment, block the profile, move on.
  • A real customer with a real complaint: answer publicly, once, short and professional — then take it to DM. Handled well in public, this sells harder than any post you could write.

Hiding beats deleting. The person who wrote it still sees it, so they don't escalate — but nobody else does.

The scoreboard

What to actually watch

Forget impressions. Four numbers.

  • Follower growth — is the top of funnel working?
  • Cost per follower — total boost spend ÷ new followers
  • Engagement on follower-targeted posts — is the middle actually warm?
  • Calls and booked jobs that mention Facebook — ask every caller, write it down. This is the only number that pays you.

Give it 90 days before you judge the strategy. Judge individual posts weekly and kill the ones that flop.

Failure modes

Five ways this gets broken

  1. Selling in Stage 1. The magnet post has a phone number and a coupon on it. Congratulations, you built a bad ad.
  2. Sharing instead of reposting. Kills your reach and you can't boost it.
  3. Boosting hazard posts to strangers. Out of context you look like you're scaring people for money. Those are for followers only.
  4. Going quiet after month one. The audience you paid to build goes cold and you got nothing for it.
  5. Making it about you. Every post answers "why does a homeowner care?" — not "look what we did."

Get moving

Your first 30 days

Week 1
Clean up the business page — hours, service area, photos, a real profile picture. Build the rebate or energy-savings guide. Check the facts.
Week 2
Post the magnet. Turn on the $10/day boost. Leave it alone.
Week 3
Post your first trust piece. Boost to followers at $4/day.
Week 4
Post your first hazard breakdown. Boost to followers. Check follower count and cost per follower.
Day 30
Decide. Is the magnet working? If yes, keep it running and start building the Stage 3 offer. If not, write a better magnet — the problem is the content, not the strategy.
