The trap

There are two kinds of viral, and one of them doesn't pay you

Read this before you copy a single post idea

That 1.29 million impression post is trade humor. Electricians laughing at electricians. It built a following fast — but the people it pulled in were other electricians, from everywhere.

That's perfect for us. Electricians are who we serve. It is close to worthless for you, because an electrician in another state is never going to book a panel upgrade from you.

If you run a residential service business, the audience you need is homeowners inside your service radius. Trade memes will get you the biggest numbers you've ever seen on a post, and none of them will answer the phone.

So use both — but know which job each one is doing:

Trade posts
Huge reach, cheap engagement, recruiting value. Good for hiring, reputation among peers, and your personal page. Do not boost these to your service area expecting calls.
Homeowner posts
Smaller numbers. Slower. These are the ones that turn into booked work. Boost these.

The test is one question: would a homeowner in my town share this with their neighbour? If the joke only lands for someone who owns a set of Kleins, it's a trade post.

Selling to homeowners? Start there instead

This guide is built on trade-audience data, because that's the audience we have. The bank · Stage 1

Magnets — posts that pull homeowners in

These map to Stage 1 of the Venus Flytrap. Job: reach strangers in your service area and turn them into followers. No selling.

Best bet

The transformation reveal

This is the category you already spotted with the under-cabinet lighting post — and it's the strongest homeowner format there is. It's visual, instant, and it makes people imagine it in their own kitchen.

01Under-cabinet lighting, lights off → lights on

Same camera position, same framing, one shot dark and one lit. No talking. The whole post is the reveal.

Shot: tripod or set the phone on the counter so the two frames line up exactly. The alignment is what makes it satisfying.
02The ugly panel → the clean panel

Rat's nest of mixed conductors on the left, labelled and dressed on the right. Electricians love this. So do homeowners — they just read it as "competent."

Shot: straight on, dead centre, same distance both times. Wipe the deadfront before the after shot.
03Outdated fixture → modern fixture

Builder-grade boob light to a flush LED. Cheap job, massive visual delta, and the most common thing a homeowner can picture wanting.

Shot: get the ceiling line in both frames so the room is recognisable.
04Exterior lighting at dusk

Before at dusk, after at dusk. Soffit lighting and path lights photograph beautifully and read as "my house could look like that."

Shot: shoot both at the same time of evening. Golden hour flatters everything.
High share rate

The horror find

The stuff you pull out of walls. These travel because people genuinely cannot believe it. Handle with care — see the warning below.

05The burned receptacle or scorched panel

What you found, what caused it, and how close it got. Calm and factual beats dramatic every time.

Caption ends with: what a homeowner can check themselves in 30 seconds. Give them something to do.
06"Wires held together by hope"

Backstab failures, wire nuts full of corrosion, a splice buried in a wall with no box. Name the specific thing. Vague danger posts don't travel.

Shot: get in close enough that the damage is unmistakable to someone who knows nothing.
07The previous-owner special

Extension cord in the wall. Outlet behind drywall. Lamp cord feeding a garage. Homeowners share these because they're all quietly worried about what the last guy did.

Angle: "this passed inspection in 1974" lands better than "this could kill you."
08Named-brand panel education

Zinsco, Federal Pacific, Challenger. Show one, explain what it is and why it matters, tell them how to check the label on theirs.

Why it works: it gives them a specific thing to go look at. That's what drives comments.
The line you don't cross

Educating and fearmongering look identical from ten feet away. The difference is whether you tell them what to do. A post that ends with "check the brand name on your panel door" is education. One that ends with "your family is in danger, call now" is fear, and homeowners can smell it.

Get the facts right too. Electrical fires are a top-three cause of house fires, not number one. One commenter with Google will take your credibility apart in public.

Underrated

Satisfying craft

09Perfectly dressed panel, no commentary

Just the photo and one line. Neat wiring is quietly one of the most-shared things in the trades, and homeowners read it as proof without needing to understand it.

10The labelled directory

A legible, typed panel schedule. Every homeowner who has ever guessed at a breaker wants this.

Caption: "We label every panel we touch. Takes 10 minutes. Saves you 10 years of guessing."
11Trenching, conduit runs, clean terminations

Straight lines and right angles. Low effort to shoot, consistently well received.

Local reach

Local and seasonal hooks

These are the ones a trade meme can never do: they only make sense to people who live where you work. That's exactly why they're worth more to you.

12The local rebate roundup

Every rebate and energy credit available in your state and utility district this year, in one post. This is the Stage 1 magnet from Guide No. 01. Nobody else bothers to make it.

Build it: have Claude or ChatGPT pull your state and utility programs together — then verify every number yourself before posting.
13Storm season / outage prep

Timed to your actual weather. What to do when the power goes out, what a generator interlock really costs, why the neighbour's setup is dangerous.

14Holiday load reality check

Why the breaker trips every December. Genuinely useful, seasonal, and it books work.

15"We're working in [neighbourhood] this week"

Name the subdivision. Homeowners in that subdivision will find it. This is the cheapest local targeting there is.

Comment driver

The question post

One of our own posts is instructive here. A "what would you do in this situation?" diagnostic puzzle did only 6,245 impressions — but pulled 30 comments, a far higher comment rate than posts with 200× the reach. Questions don't travel far, but they wake up the people who already follow you.

16"What's the oldest thing in your house that's still wired in?"

Homeowner version. Costs nothing, easy to answer, and every answer is a soft lead.

17"Guess what this cost to fix"

Photo of a repair, price in the comments the next day. Price-anchors an entire audience without quoting anybody.

18"Which one of these is in your panel?"

Four breaker brands in a grid. Sends people to go look — and some of them will not like what they find.

The bank · Stage 2

Trust posts — boosted to followers only

Small budget, warm audience. These don't need reach; they need the right people to see them.

19Award and readers'-choice wins

Local recognition, photographed properly. Post it natively on the business page — never share it across from your personal profile.

20The screenshotted review

One real review, readable, with a line about the job behind it.

21Meet the tech who's coming to your house

Name, face, how long they've been with you, one human detail. This kills more booking anxiety than anything else you'll post.

22The truck, the crew, the shop

Proof you're a real business with real people, not a phone number.

23Community involvement

The team you sponsor, the fundraiser, the volunteer day. Local credibility compounds.

24"Here's what we found and what we did"

A full job walkthrough, start to finish, no offer attached. Shows your process and sets expectations before anyone calls.

The bank · Stage 3

Offer posts — the ask

By now they've had value, seen your work, and seen what a bad panel looks like. Run these in a defined window and stop.

25Panel Awareness Month

Name the specific brands you're pulling out of houses locally, then offer inspections or replacements. Themed, time-boxed, credible.

26Flat-rate safety inspection

One clear price, one clear scope. No "call for pricing."

27Seasonal tie-in offer

Storm season, AC season, holiday lighting. Tied to something real that's happening in their life right now.

28The rebate-deadline post

A real program with a real expiry. Urgency you didn't have to invent.

If you want the trade reach anyway

Memes and text posts

These are what actually went viral for us. They work — just be clear-eyed that they pull electricians, not customers. Best home for them is your personal page, where reach is free.

29The "who wired it before us" format

Short, relatable, slightly self-deprecating trade truth over a plain background. This exact format is our single best-performing post ever.

The mechanic: the image carries the joke, the caption carries your message. People share the image. They read the caption because they shared it.
30One-trip pride

Loaded up like a pack mule to avoid a second trip. Universally understood in the trade. Ours did 19,005 impressions with no promotion.

31The diagnostic dilemma

A real field scenario with two defensible answers. Electricians argue in the comments for three days.

32Tool loyalty

Brand allegiance is a religion in this trade. Low effort, high engagement, zero business value — use it to warm a cold page.

How to write it

The caption formula that carried our best post

Image
Carries the emotion. Funny, shocking, or satisfying. It does the sharing.
First line
One line that lands on its own, because that's all most people see before "see more."
Body
The real message. Short lines, lots of white space. Ours ran nine sentences and still worked.
Close
A question or a follow ask. Not a phone number, not in Stage 1.
Links
None. Every linked post on our page died in the low hundreds. If you need a link, put it in the first comment.

Failure modes

Five ways good content gets wasted

  1. Boosting trade humour to your service area. You'll pay to entertain electricians in four states. Biggest numbers you've ever seen, zero booked work.
  2. Putting a YouTube link in the post. Confirmed on our own page across two full months — linked posts got buried every time.
  3. Reels by default. Every image post on our page outperformed every Reel. Shoot video when video is the point; otherwise post the photo.
  4. Sharing from your personal page. A share is a dead post. Re-upload it natively on the business page or it can't be boosted and won't be seen.
  5. Posting the hazard photo with no takeaway. Without "here's what to check," it reads as fear. With it, it reads as the most useful thing in their feed that day.

Honest limits

What this guide is not

Where the numbers come from

Every figure in the table is measured — pulled from our own Million Dollar Electrician page for June through August 2026. What that data proves is how a trade audience behaves: images over Reels, humour over lessons, links kill reach. Those mechanics hold up broadly.

The homeowner post ideas are a different thing. They're reasoned from the funnel logic and from what the trade data implies — they are not measured, because we don't run a residential service page. Treat this as a tested method with an untested content list.

So test it properly, and it stops being a guess inside a month:

  • Pick four magnets from Stage 1. Post one a week. Don't boost yet.
  • After four weeks, look at shares and follower growth — not likes.
  • Take the one clear winner and put the $10/day behind it for 30 days.
  • Everything that flopped, delete from your list. Everything that moved, make three more of.

That's the whole loop. You'll know more about what your market shares than any guide could tell you — and then the bank above is just a starting list you've replaced with better ones.
